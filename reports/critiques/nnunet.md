# Critique: nnU-Net

**Paper:** Isensee et al. — "nnU-Net: a self-adapting framework for
U-Net-based medical image segmentation", arXiv 1809.10486, 2018
**Read:** 24th October

## Claim

nnU-Net is a framework around the plain U-Net, not a new architecture.
It takes a new dataset and automatically configures everything the
original U-Net paper left to the user — preprocessing, patch size,
batch size, normalization, loss, augmentation, inference, post-processing
— by measuring properties of the dataset (image size, voxel spacing,
intensity statistics). It then picks between three pre-defined variants
(2D U-Net, 3D U-Net, 3D Cascade) based on validation Dice. Won the
Medical Segmentation Decathlon's 7 phase-1 tasks with no per-dataset
manual tuning, arguing that non-architectural choices matter more than
architecture tweaks.

## Evidence

*Mechanism.* The paper's contribution is not a new network. It's the
automatic configuration pipeline around three existing ones. The three
variants cover three practical cases: 2D for anisotropic slices, 3D for
isotropic volumes that fit in memory, and the Cascade (two-stage 3D) for
volumes too large for a single 3D pass — the first stage segments a
downsampled volume for coarse context, the second refines at full
resolution. Per-dataset settings (patch size, batch size, number of
pooling ops) come from the median image shape; the whole configuration
table in Table 1 is generated by these rules, not tuned by hand. The
paper's central claim is that when these non-architectural choices are
made properly, published architecture tweaks (residual, dense, attention)
provide no measurable gain over plain U-Net.

*Data.* The Medical Segmentation Decathlon: 7 phase-1 datasets covering
Brain (MRI), Heart (MRI), Liver (CT), Hippocampus (MRI), Prostate (MRI),
Lung (CT), Pancreas (CT). Varied sizes — from 36×50×35 for Hippocampus
to 482×512×512 for Liver. Three additional hidden phase-2 datasets were
evaluated after submission. All soft-tissue organs. No bone, no
musculoskeletal task.

*Loss.* Combination of Dice loss and cross-entropy: L = L_dice + L_CE.
Dice loss is a multi-class soft formulation of the standard Dice
coefficient, summed over classes and averaged over voxels. Chosen over
cross-entropy alone because medical segmentation has heavy class
imbalance (background voxels vastly outnumber foreground). No custom
weight map like U-Net's original — instead the paper uses loss
combination plus targeted patch sampling (one-third of patches forced
to contain foreground) to address the same imbalance problem.

## Weakness

1. **No bone data.** All seven Decathlon datasets are soft-tissue organs
   (brain, heart, liver, hippocampus, prostate, lung, pancreas). Bone is
   structurally different in the ways that matter for segmentation: high
   HU values, sharp cortical boundaries, thin cortical shells, and
   femur/tibia contact at the knee. The paper's claim that nnU-Net
   generalizes without per-dataset tuning is supported for soft tissue
   but not tested on bone.

2. **Design choices not validated.** The paper admits (Discussion, last
   paragraph) that "the added benefit of many of our design choices,
   such as the use of Leaky ReLUs instead of regular ReLUs and the
   parameters of our data augmentation were not properly validated."
   This matters because the paper's *central* argument is that
   non-architectural choices matter — but it does not ablate those
   choices, so we don't know how much of the gain comes from the
   configuration *rules* versus the specific defaults the authors
   picked (Leaky ReLU, instance normalization, Adam, augmentation
   parameters).

## What I would test

1. **For bone data.** Apply nnU-Net unmodified to a lower-limb CT
   dataset, with patient-level train/validation/test split at the
   level of the whole femur and tibia (not per-slice). Report
   per-bone Dice and 95th-percentile surface distance in millimetres,
   with special attention to the knee joint region where the two bones
   touch. If Dice on the joint region drops by more than a stated
   threshold (for example 0.05) compared to the shaft, the framework
   needs adaptation for bone; if it holds, the soft-tissue generalisation
   extends to bone unchanged.

2. **For unvalidated design choices.** Ablate one choice at a time on
   the same Decathlon datasets: replace Leaky ReLU with plain ReLU,
   replace instance normalization with batch normalization, remove
   elastic deformation from augmentation, remove the forced-foreground
   patch sampling. Report Dice change per dataset. If any substitution
   drops Dice by more than 0.01 on average, the paper's claim that "only
   the non-architectural aspects matter" is incomplete — some specific
   non-architectural *choices* matter too, and the paper does not say
   which.
